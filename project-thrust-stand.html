Sheet 02.2 — Electronics & Test

Propeller & Rocket Motor Thrust Test Stand

A rig for measuring real thrust output from small motors, built around an Arduino, a load cell, and a set of 3D-printed mounts — with a second variant adapted to log small rocket motor burns.

Hardware + Data
RoleMechanical design, electronics, firmware, analysis
ToolsArduino, load cell + amplifier, brushless motor/ESC
Duration3 weeks
StatusComplete, two variants
aOverview

I saw a video about toroidal propellers and was interested to experiment with their properties. As a result I have made a fully integrated propeller test rig which is able to measure the thrust of different propeller designs for a specific rpm.

bMechanical Design

I designed and 3D printed the motor mount myself, taking care to ensure the rigidity of the mount and that the thrust is transferred directly along the sensitive axis. The load cell was clamped onto the edge of a table using an F-clamp. To improve accuracy I could make an actual base to screw the load cell into, improving rigidity. I also designed a mount to test hobby rocket motors with a thicker wall to reduce melting in the mount and holes on the bottom for the exhaust. I haven’t been able to test it yet due to the risk of wild fires.

cElectronics & Firmware

I used an arduino uno microcontroller to run the rig. It was connected to the HX711 load cell amplifier to get clear and accurate thrust data. It was also wired to an electronic speed controller and brushless motor to control the rpm of the motor. The microcontroller sends different input speeds and then records the thrust to create a graph. I am planning to add a microphone so that it can also measure thrust to noise as that is a key advantage of toroidal propellers.

dResults

Results were plotted as thrust vs input signal. I found that the toroidal propeller was more efficient however that may be because I found an optimised design for the toroidal propeller whereas the standard propeller was a less optimal design which I made myself.

eReflection

One major challenge during this project was inaccurate load cell readings. Initially I thought this was due to electromagnetic fields disrupting the load cell and while this is possible the small size of the motor and the fact there are many fields of opposing polarity means the fields cancel out and the effect is very small. I realised that the problem was actually that my calibration method was very inaccurate. I learnt a lot about working through a problem systematically as well as of course how engineering systems are tested.
